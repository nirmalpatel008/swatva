Know where your students’ data lives.

Swatva is a free, open-source tool that finds the personal data in a college’s own databases and spreadsheets, so it can get ready for India’s data protection law. It runs on your machine. Nothing is uploaded.

13 May 2027

The day most duties under the DPDP Act become enforceable.

Every college has to answer for the data it keeps

Colleges hold years of Aadhaar and APAAR IDs, scholarship bank details, caste and income certificates, marks and phone numbers. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, each of them will have to:

  • Tell students what it collects and why
  • Keep that data safe from leaks and misuse
  • Report a breach within 72 hours
  • Let students see, correct or delete their data

There’s no exemption for being small. Large institutions will buy software and hire consultants. A college with a two-person IT team can’t, and the first question it struggles with is the simplest one: where is our personal data?

What it finds

Point Swatva at SQLite databases, CSV files, Excel workbooks or a whole folder. It checks a sample of every table and tells you which columns hold Aadhaar, APAAR, PAN, mobile numbers, email, bank details, dates of birth, caste or category, income, disability status, addresses and names, in English, Gujarati and Hindi.

It also flags records of people under 18, who need verifiable parental consent, and finds the phone numbers and emails that staff typed into remarks columns. That’s the data people forget when they delete a student’s records.

Built to be safe to run

Nothing leaves your machine

No cloud, no upload, no tracking. Swatva makes no network calls at all.

Read-only

Files and databases are opened for reading. Swatva never changes your data.

Masked reports

Reports never show full identifiers, so you can share one with your principal or auditor.

The plan

Swatva grows in four steps, built with the colleges that use it. Their IT staff decide what it checks first.

  1. Find

    Map where personal data lives across databases and files.

    Available now

  2. Track

    Keep a change log of personal data, so a college can show what happened to a record and when.

    2027

  3. Act

    Answer a student’s request to see or delete their data in one search, and walk staff through a breach report.

    2027

  4. Explain

    Generate plain-language notices and consent forms in English, Gujarati and Hindi.

    2027

Try it on a sample college

You need Python 3.9 or newer. The sample data is entirely made up, so you can see the full report without touching real records.

git clone https://github.com/nirmalpatel008/swatva.git
cd swatva
pip install -e ".[excel]"
python scripts/make_sample_data.py
swatva scan sample_data
# then open swatva-report/swatva-report.html

Swatva (स्वत्व) means what is one’s own. Free and open source under the MIT license. It helps you prepare and is not legal advice.
