Know where your students’ data lives.

Swatva is a free, open-source tool that finds the personal data in a college’s own databases and spreadsheets, so it can get ready for India’s data protection law. It runs on your machine. Nothing is uploaded.

Try it in your browser See a sample report

From 13 May 2027, every college has to answer for the data it keeps

Colleges hold years of Aadhaar and APAAR IDs, scholarship bank details, caste and income certificates, marks and phone numbers. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 puts four duties on each of them.

  • Tell students what it collects and why
  • Keep that data safe from leaks and misuse
  • Report a breach within 72 hours
  • Let students see, correct or delete their data

There’s no exemption for being small. Large institutions will buy software and hire consultants. A college with a two-person IT team can’t, and the first question it struggles with is the simplest one: where is our personal data?

Read the law itself: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 on India Code, the DPDP Rules, 2025 on MeitY’s site, and the government’s plain-language explainer from PIB.

What it finds

Point Swatva at SQLite databases, CSV files, Excel workbooks or a whole folder. It checks a sample of every table and tells you which columns hold Aadhaar, APAAR, PAN, mobile numbers, email, bank details, dates of birth, caste or category, income, disability status, addresses and names, in English, Gujarati and Hindi.

It also flags records of people under 18, who need verifiable parental consent, and finds the phone numbers and emails that staff typed into remarks columns. That’s the data people forget when they delete a student’s records.

Try it on a sample college

The quickest way is in your browser: choose an Excel or CSV file and see the report straight away. Nothing is uploaded.

To run it on your own computer, you need Python 3.10 or newer. The sample files are entirely made up, so you can see the full report without touching real records.

Download the sample files

Unzip them, then run:

pip install git+https://github.com/nirmalpatel008/swatva.git openpyxl
python -m swatva scan swatva-sample-data
# then open swatva-report/swatva-report.html

The download includes a fake college database, office CSV exports, and an Excel student list kept the way offices really keep them, with a title above the headers and a second sheet in Gujarati. When you’re ready, point the same command at your own files. Nothing leaves your machine.

Questions colleges ask

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. The installed tool makes no network calls. The browser version downloads Swatva’s code the first time, then reads your file inside the page. Your file is never sent to a server, including ours.

Will running Swatva make our college DPDP compliant?

No. It tells you where personal data sits, which is the first thing you need to know. Compliance also means giving notices, getting consent, securing the data and answering students’ requests. Swatva doesn’t give legal advice, so check your plan with a lawyer.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Swatva is open source under the MIT license, so you can use it, change it and share it, including inside your college’s own systems.

Which files can it read?

Excel workbooks (.xlsx), CSV files and SQLite databases, in English, Gujarati and Hindi. For an old .xls file, open it in Excel and save it as .xlsx first.

If your records sit in MySQL, an ERP or Tally, export the tables to CSV or Excel and scan the exports.

How accurate is it?

It reads up to 1,000 rows from each table and checks both the column name and the values. Aadhaar numbers must pass the same checksum UIDAI uses, so a random 12-digit number isn’t flagged. Names are harder to spot from values alone and depend more on column headers.

Treat the report as a list to check, not a final answer. When Swatva isn’t sure about a column, the report says so.

Is it safe to run on our live database?

Swatva only reads. It never writes, changes or deletes anything. To be extra careful, run it on a copy or an export.

Who builds it?

Nirmal Chhodvadiya, a data engineer in Surat who spent three years on the AWS India Big Data team. Swatva is built in the open with the colleges that use it.

How can I help?

Run it on your college’s files and tell us what it missed or got wrong by opening an issue on GitHub. Column names from your own office files, in any language, help the most. Code and translations are welcome too.
